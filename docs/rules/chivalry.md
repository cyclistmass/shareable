# Age of Chivalry

3 or 4 players. The first to **13 points** on their own turn wins. Everything in [base.md](base.md) holds, except for
what this page changes.

## What's different at a glance

- There are **no Fortune cards** and no Strongest Watch. The Grand Highway still counts.
- **Commodities** come from Towns: Parchment, Fabric and Silver.
- Towns **improve** along three tracks (Guilds, Crown, Academy) using commodities. Improvements earn **progress cards**,
  a **Capital** for the first to level 4, and a power at level 3.
- **Knights** guard the island against the **Raiders**, a ship that sails closer with every roll.
- The **Bandit** waits on the desert until the Raiders land for the first time.
- A third die, the **event die**, is rolled with the two number dice.

## Setting up

- Placement goes in turn order, then in reverse, as in the base game.
- Your first piece is a Homestead and your **second is a Town**, each followed by a road. The Town keeps the same
  spacing rule.
- The Town pays one **resource** (never a commodity) for each producing tile it touches.

## Points

| Source | Points |
|---|---|
| Homestead | 1 |
| Town | 2 |
| Capital | 2 more (a Capital is worth 4 in all) |
| Grand Highway | 2 |
| Defender of the Realm (one per card) | 1 |
| Printing Press, Charter of Rights | 1 each |
| Holding the Trader | 1 |

## Commodities and production

The bank holds 12 of each commodity.

| A Town on | Pays |
|---|---|
| Woodland | 1 Timber + 1 Parchment |
| Meadow | 1 Fleece + 1 Fabric |
| Quarry | 1 Stone + 1 Silver |
| Clay pit | 2 Clay |
| Grainfield | 2 Grain |

A Homestead still pays 1 resource. When the bank runs short, the base game's rule applies to each card type.

## The dice

Each roll is two number dice (one red, one ivory) and the event die.

| Event die | Faces | Does |
|---|---|---|
| Raiders' ship | 3 | The ship sails one step closer. On the 7th step it lands (see [The Raiders](#the-raiders)). |
| Guilds gate | 1 | Players with a Guilds level may draw a Guilds card. |
| Crown gate | 1 | Players with a Crown level may draw a Crown card. |
| Academy gate | 1 | Players with an Academy level may draw an Academy card. |

**Gates:** when a gate comes up, every player with level 1 or more in that track draws a card from its deck if the
red die is no higher than their level + 1:

| Level | Draws on red |
|---|---|
| 1 | 1–2 |
| 2 | 1–3 |
| 3 | 1–4 |
| 4 | 1–5 |
| 5 | always |

Players draw in turn order, starting with the roller. An empty deck gives nothing.

**Order of a roll:**

1. The event die: the ship moves (and may land), or a gate draws cards.
2. Anyone over the progress-card limit discards.
3. Any Towns lost to the Raiders are given up.
4. Any rewards for beating the Raiders are drawn.
5. On a number other than 7: production, then the Academy level-3 power. On a 7: discards, then the Bandit, if it's
   loose.

## Improving your Towns

Each track has five levels, paid in its commodity. Level *n* costs *n* cards:

| Track | Commodity | Level-3 power |
|---|---|---|
| Guilds | Fabric | Trade commodities with the bank 2 for 1, for any card. |
| Crown | Silver | Knights may be promoted to mighty. |
| Academy | Parchment | On a roll other than 7 that pays you nothing at all, take 1 resource of your choice from the bank. |

- You need at least one Town on the board to improve.
- **Capitals:** the first player to reach level 4 in a track turns one of their Towns into that track's Capital,
  worth 2 more points.
- A player who reaches level 5 takes the Capital from a holder still below 5. If levels are tied, it stays put.
- The Raiders can't take a Capital.
- **How the app plays it:** an improvement that would win a Capital can't be made if you have no plain Town to put it
  on.

## Progress cards

54 cards, 18 in each of the Guilds, Crown and Academy decks. Every card is in [cards.md](cards.md).

- **Hand limit: 4.** The Printing Press and Charter of Rights don't count toward it: they are shown and scored the
  moment they're drawn.
- Whenever you hold more than 4, you discard one of your choice straight away, even on your own turn.
- **When to play:** as many as you like, on your own turn, after rolling, even the turn you drew them. The one
  exception is the Transmuter, which is played before rolling, instead of rolling.
- Free roads (Pathfinders, Envoy) and free promotions (Forge) are used at once or turned down. Nothing else happens
  while they're pending.
- Played and discarded cards go face down under their deck. The point cards leave the game for good.

## Knights

Each player has 6 knights: 2 basic (strength 1), 2 strong (2) and 2 mighty (3).

| Do | Costs | |
|---|---|---|
| Raise a knight | 1 Fleece, 1 Stone | A basic knight, unsupplied, on an empty corner on your roads. The spacing rule doesn't apply. |
| Supply a knight | 1 Grain | It counts against the Raiders and can act, but not on the turn it was supplied. |
| Promote a knight | 1 Fleece, 1 Stone | Basic to strong; strong to mighty only with Crown level 3. You need a free knight of the higher strength; the old one goes back to your supply. |

- A knight can be promoted once per turn, supplied or not, and keeps its supply.
- **Acting** uses up the knight's supply, so each knight acts at most once before it's supplied again:
  - **Move:** along your own roads, past your own pieces but not other players', to any empty corner.
  - **Drive off:** move onto a corner held by another player's weaker knight. That player moves their knight along
    their own roads to an empty corner it can reach, or it goes back to their supply if there's none.
  - **Chase the Bandit:** a knight on a corner of the Bandit's tile moves the Bandit and takes a card, as on a 7. Only
    once the Bandit is loose.
- Knights block other players' roads like buildings do, and break their Grand Highway.

## Ramparts

- 2 Clay. At most 3 per player, and one per Town (a Capital can have one).
- Each Ramparts lets you hold 2 more cards before a 7 costs you half.
- A Town lost to the Raiders loses its Ramparts.

## The Bandit

- The Bandit stays on the desert until the Raiders land for the first time, win or lose.
- Until then a 7 still makes players over the limit give back half, but nobody is robbed.
- Commodities count as cards for a 7, and the Bandit can take them.

## Trading

| With the bank | Rate |
|---|---|
| Resources | 4:1, 3:1 at a 3-for-1 harbour, 2:1 at that resource's harbour, or 2:1 for the tile the Trader stands on, while it's yours |
| Commodities | 4:1, 3:1 at a 3-for-1 harbour, 2:1 with Guilds level 3 |
| Swift Hulls | the named card 2:1 for the rest of the turn |

You can take commodities from the bank, and trades between players can include them.

## The Raiders

- The ship sails 7 steps. When it lands it attacks, then starts over.
- **Raiders' strength:** the number of Towns on the island, Capitals included.
- **The island's strength:** the total strength of every supplied knight.

**If the knights are as strong or stronger**, the island holds:

- The one player who gave the most strength gets a **Defender of the Realm** card (1 point; 6 in the box).
- If several players tie for most, or those cards have run out, each of them draws a progress card from a deck of their
  choice.
- If nobody gave any strength, nobody is rewarded.

**If the Raiders are stronger:**

- Among the players with at least one plain Town, those who gave the least strength each lose one Town of their choice.
- The lost Town becomes a Homestead again and loses its Ramparts.
- A player whose Towns are all Capitals is safe.

**Either way:**

- Every knight loses its supply.
- After the first landing, the Bandit is loose.

## How the app plays it

- A Town lost to the Raiders when you have no Homestead left in your supply is taken off the board.
- Turncoat can give you a mighty knight without Crown level 3.
- Knights supplied by the Marshal can't act that turn, the same as knights you pay to supply.
- The Hoist is used straight away as an improvement that costs one commodity less.
- Waterworks, Deep Shafts, Wrecker, Feast and Marshal are only offered when they would do something.
